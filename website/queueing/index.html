🏒 1. The hockey stick

For one barista with random arrivals and service (M/M/1), the wait in line is ρ / (1 − ρ) service times. Going from 80% to 90% load more than doubles it (4 → 9); from 90% to 95% it doubles again (9 → 19). A café that is "only" 95% busy has a long line.

🎲 2. Variability makes lines

Kingman's approximation: Wq ≈ ρ/(1 − ρ) · (ca² + cs²)/2 · service time, where c² measures how irregular arrivals and services are (random 1, steady ¼, clockwork 0). Make both clockwork and the line disappears, even at 95% load.

🤝 3. Pooling

With several baristas at the same load, one shared line beats a line per barista: nobody waits while another barista sits idle. And more baristas at the same load means shorter waits (economies of scale).

🎮 Staffing challenge: run the café for a day

Capacity planning · wages vs waiting

You manage a café from 7:00 to 15:00. Choose how many baristas work each hour. Every barista costs a wage, and every minute a customer waits in line costs goodwill. Too few baristas and the line explodes; too many and you pay people to stand around. Find the cheapest day in total.

The rules

  1. 📅Demand varies The game code sets the expected customers per hour, with a breakfast and a lunch rush. Arrivals are random.
  2. 🧑‍🍳Plan the shifts Pick 1 to 8 baristas per hour. Each costs $18 per hour.
  3. ⏳Waiting costs Each minute a customer waits in line costs $0.30. Customers in line at closing are still served.
  4. ☕Open the café The day is simulated with the same customers for every plan, so you can compare plans fairly.
